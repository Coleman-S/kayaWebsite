About Rome

Rome, Italy's capital, is a sprawling, cosmopolitan city with nearly 3,000 years of globally influential art, architecture and culture on display. Ancient ruins such as the Forum and the Colosseum evoke the power of the former Roman Empire.

Founded in 753 BC by Romulus, Rome was the capital of the Roman Kingdom, the Roman Republic and the Roman Empire. It served as the center of one of the greatest civilizations ever known, which dominated the Mediterranean region for over 1,000 years.

Vatican City, headquarters of the Roman Catholic Church, lies within Rome's boundaries, making it the only example of a country within a city. Rome's historic center is listed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site, and the city is particularly notable for its museums, architecture, religious buildings, and archaeological sites.
