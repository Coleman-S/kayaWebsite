About Valletta

Valletta is the capital city of Malta, colloquially known as Il-Belt (The City) in Maltese. It is located on the island of Malta and is the southernmost capital of Europe, as well as the smallest capital city in the European Union by area.

Founded in 1566 by the Knights of St. John, Valletta is a UNESCO World Heritage site and was one of the most modern cities of its time. The city was named after Jean Parisot de Valette, the Grand Master who led the Order to victory during the Great Siege of Malta against the Ottoman Empire in 1565.

Valletta's 16th-century buildings were constructed by the Knights Hospitaller. The city is known for its museums, palaces, grand churches, and Baroque landmarks. Its grid of narrow streets boasts some of Europe's finest artworks, churches, and palaces.
