About Venice

Venice, known as "La Serenissima," is a city built on more than 100 small islands in a lagoon in the Adriatic Sea. It has no roads, just canals – including the Grand Canal thoroughfare – lined with Renaissance and Gothic palaces.

The city was historically the capital of the Venetian Republic and was a major maritime power during the Middle Ages and Renaissance, as well as an important center of commerce (especially silk, grain, and spice) and art in the 13th century up to the end of the 17th century.

Venice is renowned for its beautiful architecture, artworks, and its unique cityscape. The city and its lagoon are a UNESCO World Heritage site.
